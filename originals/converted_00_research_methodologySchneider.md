# VI Research Design

---
**Folie 20**

Empirical research / text

The research question is answered in a hypothesis that is tested using data
Hypothesis is a formulation of the type
If…, then…
The more …, the more (less)…
In order to test a hypothesis, a relation between the first and second term of the sentence is needed
What is to be explained – dependent variables
What explains – independent variables
Empirical research needs data, and therefore data collection

---
**Folie 21**

Additional remarks (i) – data as descriptors

Hypothesis tests theories

Negative Test: Hypothesis is rejected  Theory is rejected
Positive test: Hypothesis cannot be rejected  Theory cannot be rejected

«cannot be rejected» does not prove «truth»
«cannot be rejected is not a demonstration
«cannot be rejected» means: the theory works as a working horse

---
**Folie 22**

Additional remarks (ii) – data as prognosis

Estimation of Parameters

The metrics of a theory can be estimated using statistical methods (past data)

Prognosis

“Cannot be rejected” theories can be metricized with estimated parameters
This leads to an abstract model
The model can lead to a statement about the future, prognosis (future data)

Analytical research / text

The research question is answered with a thesis
A thesis is a proposition stated or put forward for consideration, especially one to be discussed and proved or to be maintained against objections.
In order to consider a thesis, you need

Argument in favor
Fact supporting argument
Argument against
Fact supporting argument against
Argument in favor that counterbalances /addresses argument against
Fact supporting…
…
Conclusion

**Folie 24**

Comparison

The research question is answered with a comparison
A comparison addresses at least two objects by opposing them using criteria
Criteria are rules or principles for evaluating something
The comparison
Chooses criteria
Explaining why they were chosen
Sometimes, criteria are weighted.
Examines each object in light of each criterion
Summarizes findings

---
**Folie 25**

Essay

The research question is answered with the development of a (new) theory or model
Often, essays are not scientific texts, since they have persuasive objectives
Often, essays have practical goals
Essays use examples, case-studies, etc. to make the case for something
The case made answers the research question

**Folie 32**

(Some) Mechanics of writing

Use direct quotations sparingly; paraphrase studies
Prioritize studies in the literature:
	Signal importance by discussing relatively more important studies with more 	detail
	Signal importance by noting influence on subsequent studies
Short paragraphs can help to keep writing crisp
	One paragraph, one idea
	Each paragraph should have a point, and only one point
	Typical structure: Topic sentence: Point of paragraph, Elaboration on the 	point, Supporting evidence for the point
Subheadings can help to clarify structure
Imagine that you are telling a story

	1, 2, 3

**Folie 38**

# XI Research Ethics
Honesty: Honestly report data, results, methods and procedures, and publication status. Do not fabricate, falsify, or misrepresent data. Do not deceive colleagues, research sponsors, or the public.
Objectivity: Strive to avoid bias in experimental design, data analysis, data interpretation, peer review, personnel decisions, grant writing, expert testimony, and other aspects of research where objectivity is expected or required. Disclose personal or financial interests that may affect research.
Carefulness: Avoid careless errors and negligence; carefully and critically examine your own work and the work of your peers. Keep good records of research activities, such as data collection, research design, and correspondence with agencies or journals.
Respect for Intellectual Property: Honor patents, copyrights, and other forms of intellectual property. Do not use unpublished data, methods, or results without permission. Give proper acknowledgement or credit for all contributions to research. Never plagiarize.

**Folie 41**

# Positivistic vs. phenomenological paradigm
| Positivistic paradigm | Phenomenological paradigm |
|---|---|
| Tends to produce quantitative data | Tends to produce qualitative data |
| Uses large samples | Uses small samples |
| Concerned with hypotheses testing | Concerned with generating theories |
| Data is highly specific and precise | Data is rich and subjective |
| The location is artificial | The location is natural |
| Reliability is high | Reliability is low |
| Validity is low | Validity is high |
| Generalises from sample to population | Generalises from one setting to another |

**Folie 42**

# Quantitative vs. qualitative research methods (RM)
**Quantitative RM:** Sampling; Structured Interviewing; Self-completion Questionnaires; Structured Observations; Content Analysis; Secondary analysis and official statistics

**Qualitative RM:** Observations; Qualitative Interviewing; Focus Groups; Language-based approaches to the collection of qualitative data (e.g. discourse and conversation analysis); Collection and qualitative analysis of texts and documents; Case studies
