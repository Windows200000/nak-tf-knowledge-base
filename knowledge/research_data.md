# Research Data – Finding, Citing, Documenting (for TFs with own or external data)
**Source:** [Forschungsdaten_DINA4_ONLINE_VER_02_06.pdf](<../originals/Forschungsdaten_DINA4_ONLINE_VER_02_06.pdf>) · full text: [txt](<../originals_text/Forschungsdaten_DINA4_ONLINE_VER_02_06.txt>)

**About the source:** ZBW, GESIS & RatSWD (2015). *Auffinden, Zitieren, Dokumentieren: Forschungsdaten in den Sozial- und Wirtschaftswissenschaften* (2. Auflage), doi:10.4232/10.fisuzida2015.2, CC-BY-SA 3.0 DE (p. 3, 24). Preface dated December 2014 (p. 5). PDF page numbers = printed page numbers. All 28 pages read (p. 1 cover, p. 2 blank, p. 4 and p. 16 figures checked as rendered images).

**Important limitations — read before relying on this file:**
- **Quantitative data only.** *„Qualitative Forschungsdaten werden in diesem Wegweiser weitgehend ausgeklammert."* (p. 7) → Interview transcripts, qualitative expert interviews etc. are barely covered.
- **Dated (2014/2015).** It predates the **DSGVO/GDPR (applicable since 25 May 2018)**; it refers to *„Bundes- und Landesdatenschutzgesetze sowie das Bundesstatistikgesetz"* (p. 12). Several portals/URLs named are outdated (e.g. quandl.com, Thomson Reuters Datastream/Data Citation Index — now under other owners; general knowledge).
- **The FAIR principles are NOT mentioned** anywhere in the document. FAIR content below is marked as general knowledge.
- Written for researchers/theses in economics & social sciences, not for company-internal practice papers; TF relevance is drawn out in the "TF application" notes (general knowledge, not from source).

The guide answers three questions (p. 4, verbatim):
1. *Wo kann ich die für meine Fragestellung geeigneten Daten finden oder muss ich mangels existierender Daten selbst Daten erheben?*
2. *Wenn ich Daten von anderen nachnutze, wie muss ich diese Daten zitieren?*
3. *Wie dokumentiere ich eigene Forschungsdaten, damit sie sinnvoll nachgenutzt werden können?*

Framework: the rules of **good scientific practice** (*gute wissenschaftliche Praxis*, DFG 1998/2013) (p. 3).

## 1. Finding existing data (p. 6–14)

### 1.1 Types of quantitative research data (Tabelle 1, p. 7)

| Type | Examples (verbatim, abridged) |
|---|---|
| **Statistische Daten** (aggregate/macro) | Statistische Ämter des Bundes und der Länder (Bildung, Finanzen, Gesundheit, Landwirtschaft, Umwelt/Energie, VGR (BIP), Zahl der Beschäftigten, Einwohner); OECD; Eurostat |
| **Survey-Daten** (micro) | ALLBUS, Community Innovation Survey, EU-LFS, EU-SILC, GLES, ifo-Geschäftsklimaindex, Innovationsmonitor des IW, Mannheimer Innovationspanel, NEPS, PIAAC, PISA, SHARE, SOEP, ZEW-Hightech-Gründungspanel … |
| **Prozessproduzierte Mikrodaten** | Öffentliche Verwaltung (Steuer-, Melde-, Kfz-Daten …); **Wirtschaftsdaten: Aktienkurse, Bilanzdaten, Strukturmerkmale**; Bundesagentur für Arbeit (Arbeitsmarkt, Sozialversicherungsdaten …) |

Definitions:
- **Makrodaten** aggregate many individual observations into one value (e.g. GDP, average household income); *disaggregierte Makrodaten* are finer (regional/sectoral) but not micro-level (p. 8).
- **Mikrodaten / Survey-Daten:** detailed information on individual units (persons, households, establishments, firms); anonymity *„durch geeignete Anonymisierung … voll gewahrt"* (p. 11).
- **Prozessproduzierte Mikrodaten** arise mostly from administrative processes, i.e. *„einem nicht primär wissenschaftlichen Hintergrund"* (p. 11).

**Company data (highly relevant to TFs, p. 11):**
> Mikrodaten zu Abläufen, Produktionsprozessen und -kosten in Unternehmen enthalten oft wettbewerbsrelevante Informationen (z. B. Entwicklung des Kundenstammes) und sind somit speziell geschützt und nur schwer zugänglich. Meist sind derart sensible Daten nur im direkten Kontakt zu Firmen auswertbar und unterliegen in jedem Fall restriktiven Vertrags- und Nutzungsbedingungen.

Also: cross-country company/official data are often collected differently by country and *„i. d. R. nicht länderübergreifend … vergleichbar"* (p. 11); comparative international studies are hard because producers use different definitions and methods (p. 8).

### 1.2 Where to look (Übersichten 1–3, p. 8–11)
- First hints: supervisor, colleagues, *„nicht zuletzt die einschlägige Literatur"* (p. 7).
- **Germany, aggregate:** Statistisches Bundesamt + Landesämter, cities/districts; Bundesagentur für Arbeit; Open-Data portals of the Länder (e.g. Hamburg, Berlin); **Statista** (commercial, partly free; compiles data from market research, business, official statistics) (p. 8–9).
- **International aggregate:** Eurostat, OECD (+ iLibrary), World Bank, UN/UNdata, FAO, ILO, IMF, UNESCO, WHO, WTO; Allthatstats, EUKlems, ICPSR, Total Economy Database, Urban Audit (p. 8–9).
- **Micro/survey data:** RatSWD-accredited **Forschungsdatenzentren (FDZ)** — RatSWD portal; GESIS data catalogue; Bundesbank FDSZ; FDZ der BA im IAB; LMU-ifo EBDC (firm data incl. Amadeus/Hoppenstedt); Mikrozensus (+ free **CAMPUS-File** for practice); SOEP; CESSDA, SHARE (p. 8, 10).
- **Firm/process data:** AFiD, KombiFiD; commercial: Amadeus, Bisnode/Hoppenstedt, Bloomberg, Bureau van Dijk, Datastream, WRDS — usually paid; check terms (AGBs) for reuse (p. 11–12).
- Link collections: AEA data sources, Erasmus University Rotterdam (p. 11).

### 1.3 Access routes (Zugangswege, p. 12–14)
1. **Aggregate statistics (Makrodaten):** mostly free, online, sometimes after registration (p. 12).
2. **Statistical microdata:** protected by data-protection law and Bundesstatistikgesetz; *„So muss die Erhebung von personenbeziehbaren Daten immer an ein bestimmtes Forschungsziel geknüpft sein, welches als so relevant erachtet wird, dass die Gewinnung personenbeziehbarer Daten zu rechtfertigen ist."* Protection is ensured by anonymisation whose degree depends on type and control of use (p. 12). Access tiers (Abbildung 1, p. 12–14), from low to high information density / access control:
   - **Public-Use-Files (PUF) / CAMPUS-Files** – fully anonymised, public, strongly reduced information (off-site).
   - **Scientific-Use-Files (SUF)** – *„faktisch anonymisiert"*, only for scientific institutions and scientific projects (off-site).
   - **Gastwissenschaftlerarbeitsplatz (GWAP)** – on-site, weakly anonymised data on secured PCs, on application.
   - **Kontrollierte Datenfernverarbeitung (KDFV)** – formally anonymised original data; researcher writes scripts on dummy data, FDZ staff run them; results released after *Geheimhaltungsprüfung* (re-identification risk check).
   - Costs: science-run data usually free for science; official FDZ microdata ca. 250 € per statistic/year/access route (state 2014) (p. 14).
3. **Commercial data:** contract and fees; ask your **library** first for licensed access (e.g. ZBW) (p. 14).

**TF application (general knowledge, not from source):** For a TF, free aggregate data (Destatis, Eurostat, OECD, BA) and CAMPUS/PUF files are realistic; SUF/GWAP/KDFV require institutional applications and time that a TF rarely allows. Statista entries should be traced back to and cited by the original publisher where possible.

## 2. Citing research data (p. 15–19)

### 2.1 Why (p. 16)
Correct data citation is part of good scientific practice and recognises data production as *„eine primäre Wissenschaftsleistung"*; data *„sollten im Sinne von »Good Scientific Practice« zitierbar sein und zitiert werden"* (Allianz der deutschen Wissenschaftsorganisationen, 2010).

### 2.2 Minimum elements (p. 16)
At least the usual bibliographic data — **Autor, Titel, Verlag, Publikationsdatum** — plus a **persistent identifier**, because digital data are *„dynamischer und fragiler"*, versions are not always identifiable and storage locations change (p. 16–17). PID systems: ARK, **DOI**, Handle, PURL, URN; the guide **recommends DOI** (p. 17). No binding standard existed (p. 16–17).

### 2.3 Recommended citation format (da|ra metadata schema 3.1 / DataCite, p. 17–18)
Basic form:
> **Creator (Veröffentlichungsdatum): Titel. Publikationsagent. Identifier.**

Extended form (with optional Version and resource type):
> **Creator (Veröffentlichungsdatum): Titel. Version. Publikationsagent. Typ der Ressource. Identifier.**

Element rules (p. 17–18):
- **Creator:** max. five names, then *et al.*
- **Veröffentlichungsdatum:** year of publication of the dataset only.
- **Titel:** title(s) of the study/dataset.
- **Version:** version number.
- **Publikationsagent:** data centre/institution that published it.
- **Typ der Ressource:** Sammlung, Datensatz, Text, Video, Bild, Audio, interaktive Ressource.
- **Identifier:** persistent identifier; DOI either with resolver URL (`http://doi.org/10.4232/1.10770`) or as hyperlinked `doi:10.4232/1.10770`.
- Look up metadata via the da|ra portal or DataCite Metadata Store (p. 18).

Examples (verbatim, p. 18):
> Fahrenberg, Jochen (2010): Freiburger Beschwerdenliste FBL. Primärdaten der Normierungsstichprobe 1993. Version 1.0.0. ZPID- Leibniz-Zentrum für Psychologische Information und Dokumentation. Dataset. http://doi.org/10.5160/psychdata.fgjn05an08
>
> Rattinger, Hans; Roßteutscher, Sigrid; Schmitt-Beck, Rüdiger; Weßels, Bernhard (2012): Wahlkampf-Panel (GLES 2009). Version: 3.0.0. GESIS Datenarchiv. Dataset. doi:10.4232/1.11131.
>
> Schupp, Jürgen; Kroh, Martin; Goebel, Jan; Bartsch, Simone; Giesselmann, Marco et. al. (2013): Sozio-oekonomisches Panel (SOEP), Daten der Jahre 1984-2012. Version: 29. SOEP- Sozio-oekonomisches Panel. Dataset. doi:10.5684/soep.v29.

Further rules:
- **Provider-specific citation requirements take precedence** — some providers (e.g. NEPS) make a specific citation mandatory in their terms of use; resolve the DOI and read the terms (p. 18).
- Journals may require their own format; the DOI Citation Formatter (DataCite + Crossref) converts to ~500 styles (p. 18). (For a TF: adapt to the NAK citation style — general knowledge.)
- **DOI structure** (Abb. 3, p. 18; Übersicht 4, p. 17): prefix always starts with `10.` and identifies the data centre; suffix identifies the object, e.g. `10.3478/33.v2`. Resolve via `http://doi.org/<DOI>`.

### 2.4 Source lines under tables and figures (Übersicht 5, p. 18)
> Es ist üblich, unter den Tabellen und Grafiken einer Publikation die Quelle anzugeben. … z.B.: »Quelle: SOEPv29.1, Berechnungen des Autors« oder »Source: SOEPv29.1, author's calculations«. Wenn ein Datensatz mit einem DOI-Namen versehen ist, dann sollte dieser auch mit angegeben werden. … Möglich wäre z. B.: »Quellen: SOEP (v29.1) (doi:10.5684/soep.v29.1), Berechnungen des Autors«. **In jedem Fall sollte die vollständige Zitationsangabe im Literaturverzeichnis aufgeführt werden.**

**TF application (general knowledge, not from source):** the same pattern applies to company data, e.g. „Quelle: Eigene Berechnung auf Basis interner Produktionsdaten der [Firma], Jan–Jun 2025" — with the internal source handled per NAK internal-source rules ([Zitieren interner Quellen.pdf](<../originals/Zitieren interner Quellen.pdf>)).

### 2.5 Replication / journal data policies (Übersicht 6, p. 19)
Some journals require submission of data **and program code** (do-file, SPS-file) for replication; in 2012 only 29 of 141 economics journals had such policies (Vlaeminck & Siegert, 2012). Replication Wiki exists for economics.

## 3. Documenting own data (p. 20–23)

Context: *„Einige Studierende oder Promovenden erheben oder sammeln im Zuge ihrer Qualifikationsarbeiten auch selbst Daten, erstellen neue Datensätze aus anderen, bereits existenten Datensätzen …"* (p. 21).

**Goal:** *„Hauptziel einer Dokumentation ist es, die Entstehung des Datensatzes nachvollziehbar zu machen und ihn so zu beschreiben, dass Dritte damit arbeiten können."* (p. 21) Motivation: good scientific practice, *Reproduzierbarkeit und Transparenz*. Effort scales with dataset size and scope of the work; sometimes **the calculation code** (do-file, sps-file) suffices to show how a dataset was built from documented primary data (p. 21). Documentation is often neglected under time pressure (p. 21).

### 3.1 The six documentation points (p. 21–23)
1. **Inhalt** – keyword description (e.g. „Arbeitsmarkt", „Wahlen", „Investitionsgüter"), standardised codes such as **JEL codes**, and preferably a short **abstract** of the data content (p. 21).
2. **Beobachtungseinheit** – the smallest unit in the dataset (countries, persons, goods …), clearly named; in "rectangular format" variables are columns, units are rows; units can be aggregated (persons → household → Land); time dimension e.g. „Personen-Jahre" (p. 21).
3. **Datengrundlage** – **Vollerhebung oder Stichprobe?** This determines which statements are possible. Example: a full survey of one school's children *cannot* support general statements about children outside that school (p. 21). For samples: define the **Grundgesamtheit** and how the sample was drawn (*Zufallsstichprobe, Quotenstichprobe, Ziehung ohne Vorgaben*); document **Bruttosample** (planned) vs. **Nettosample** (actually participated) (p. 22).
4. **Erhebungsmethode** – how the data were obtained (e.g. newspaper clippings, **interviews — quantitative or qualitative**, web search queries); needed to judge **Reliabilität (Messgenauigkeit)** and **Validität (Aussagekraft)**. Provide supporting material: *„Fragebögen, Skalenhandbüchern, Testbeschreibungen, Kodierungsvorschriften, Übersetzungshilfen oder Anschreiben … – kurzum alles, was den Prozess der Datenerstellung für den Nutzer konkretisiert."* (p. 22)
5. **Umfang** – number of observations, and more importantly **number of variables and what they measure**; for details a **Codebuch / Datenhandbuch** (example: SOEP codebook) (p. 22).
6. **Zugang** – whether and how others can obtain the data; contact person/institution; **data-protection law applies to self-collected data too** (see §4) (p. 22–23).

Additional quality indicators for surveys (p. 23): **Item-Nonresponse** (share of missing answers) and **Unit-Nonresponse** (share of non-participants). The more detailed the documentation (incl. DOI and a citation suggestion), the more likely the data are cited (p. 23).

### 3.2 Archiving / sharing own datasets (p. 23; Übersicht 7, p. 22)
- **Repositories** (*„an Universitäten oder Forschungseinrichtungen betriebene Dokumentenserver"*, p. 23 fn. 3) archive data and assign DOIs.
- Germany: **datorium** (GESIS, since 2014; self-upload, mandatory title and primary researcher, DOI, permanent availability); **DSZ-BO** Bielefeld (business and organisational data, e.g. internal employee surveys after their original purpose is fulfilled; applies extra anonymisation before sharing); **Verbund Forschungsdaten Bildung** (mainly qualitative education data); university repositories (LMU, TU Berlin, Bielefeld, Heidelberg, Mannheim — usually members only). International: ADA, DDA, DANS, DataHub, ICPSR, UK Data Archive. Directory: **re3data.org** (p. 22–23).

**TF application (general knowledge, not from source):** Public archiving of company data is normally not possible/permitted in a TF (confidentiality, Sperrvermerk). The transferable part is the *documentation discipline*: describe data origin, sample, method, variables and processing steps in the method chapter/appendix so the assessor can trace results.

## 4. Data protection & anonymisation

### 4.1 What the source says
- Collection of person-related data must be tied to a specific, sufficiently relevant research goal (p. 12).
- Protection of person-related data is a legal mandate ensured by **anonymisation**; degree depends on use and control (p. 12). Tiers: fully anonymised (PUF/CAMPUS) → *faktisch anonymisiert* (SUF) → weakly anonymised (GWAP) → formally anonymised (KDFV) (p. 12–14).
- For self-collected data: *„Beispielsweise müssen personenbezogene Daten für eine Weitergabe anonymisiert werden, d.h. sämtliche Möglichkeiten zur Identifikation von Individuen müssen entfernt werden … Dies bedeutet unter Umständen auch, bestimmte seltene Merkmale bzw. Kombinationen von Merkmalen zu entfernen. Erste Ansprechpartner für Fragen in diesem Zusammenhang sind die Datenschützer der jeweiligen Institution, die im Zweifel immer vor einer Studie mit selbst erhobenen Daten kontaktiert werden sollten."* (p. 22–23)
- Company micro-data: competition-sensitive, restrictive contract and usage terms (p. 11).
- Distinguish commercial vs. scientific users and name reuse costs when sharing (p. 23).

### 4.2 Additions for TFs (general knowledge, not from source)
- **Legal basis today is the DSGVO/GDPR + BDSG**, not only the laws named in the 2015 guide. Pseudonymised data are still personal data; only truly anonymised data fall outside GDPR.
- **Interviews/surveys with colleagues:** inform participants about purpose, voluntariness, storage and anonymisation; obtain consent (written/recorded); check whether the **Betriebsrat** (works council) must be involved for employee surveys/performance-related data; contact the company **Datenschutzbeauftragte** (mirrors the source's advice on p. 23).
- **Small-n re-identification:** in a company context, role + department + shift can identify a person even without a name — remove/aggregate rare attribute combinations (cf. p. 23 on rare characteristics); report small groups only aggregated.
- **Confidential company data:** clarify with the company what may appear in the TF; use *Sperrvermerk* if NAK/the company requires it; mask or index absolute figures (e.g. costs → relative values) if needed.
- **AI tools:** do not paste personal or confidential company data into external AI tools (see [academic_writing_guide.md](academic_writing_guide.md) §11.2, booklet p. 8).

## 5. Storage and data management (general knowledge, not from source unless page given)
The guide does not cover storage, backup or data-management plans in detail (it only mentions that DFG requires a statement on data reuse and NSF requires **Data-Management-Pläne** since 2011, p. 3, and that journals increasingly require data + syntax, p. 5, 19). Practical minimum for a TF:
- Keep raw data unchanged; work on copies; document every processing step (script, Excel formula log, or written protocol) — cf. "Berechnungscode" as documentation (p. 21).
- Store on company-approved systems; separate key lists (names ↔ IDs) from data; delete personal data when no longer needed per agreement.
- Version files (date/version in filename) so tables in the TF can be traced to a specific data state.

## 6. FAIR principles (general knowledge, not from source)
Not mentioned in this guide. For reference: research data should be **Findable** (persistent identifier, metadata), **Accessible** (clear access conditions, even if restricted), **Interoperable** (open/standard formats, standard vocabularies), **Reusable** (licence, provenance, documentation) — Wilkinson et al. (2016), *Scientific Data*. The guide's recommendations map onto FAIR: DOI/PIDs (F), *Zugang* point 6 (A), JEL codes/standard metadata schema (I), the six documentation points + code (R). For TFs with confidential company data, "A" usually means *documented but restricted*.

## Reviewer checklist (apply to a TF draft)

**External data used**
- [ ] Every dataset/statistic is cited with creator, year, title, (version), publisher/agent, (resource type), persistent identifier/URL — and listed in the bibliography (p. 16–18).
- [ ] Provider-specific citation requirements followed (p. 18).
- [ ] Every table/figure based on data has a source line (e.g. „Quelle: …, eigene Berechnungen") (p. 18).
- [ ] Original publisher cited rather than an aggregator where possible (general knowledge).
- [ ] Limits of comparability noted for cross-country/cross-source data (p. 8, 11).

**Own data (interviews, surveys, company data)**
- [ ] Content, observation unit, data basis (full survey vs. sample; population; sampling method; gross vs. net sample) described (p. 21–22).
- [ ] Collection method explained; instruments (questionnaire, interview guide, coding rules, cover letter) provided, e.g. in the appendix (p. 22).
- [ ] Scope: number of cases and variables and what they measure; nonresponse reported for surveys (p. 22–23).
- [ ] Conclusions do not overreach the data basis (e.g. one department/site ≠ whole company/industry) (p. 21 school example).
- [ ] Processing steps/calculations traceable (code, formulas, protocol) (p. 21).
- [ ] Reliability and validity discussed (p. 22).

**Protection**
- [ ] Persons not identifiable — names removed, rare attribute combinations aggregated (p. 22–23).
- [ ] Consent/data-protection/works-council questions addressed; company approval for confidential data (DSGVO, Sperrvermerk = general knowledge).
- [ ] No personal/confidential data entered into external AI tools (booklet p. 8).
