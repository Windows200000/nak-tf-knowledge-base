## Part A – The figure (transcription)

**Provenance:** a photographed book figure labelled "Abb. 3" from a work by *Prexl*. The filename only gives the surname. (Likely Lisa Prexl, *Mit digitalen Quellen arbeiten*, UTB — general knowledge, **not verified from the file**; confirm before citing the figure.)

**Structure:** a tree/mind-map with one root and three branches.

```
Wissenschaftlichkeit von Quellen bewerten
├── Relevant?
│   └── Bezug zu Fragestellung
├── Zitierfähig?
│   └── Quelle lässt sich zuverlässig auffinden
└── Zitierwürdig?
    ├── vertrauenswürdiger Autor
    ├── methodisches und strukturgeleitetes Vorgehen
    ├── wissenschaftliche Fragestellung
    ├── Qualitätssicherung durch Publikationsmedium
    └── präzise Wissenschaftssprache und Verweise auf andere Quellen
```

(Verbatim German labels. The figure shows no ordering or weighting between the three branches; "Zitierfähig?" is drawn on the left, "Relevant?" top-right, "Zitierwürdig?" bottom-right.)

**Key distinction the figure makes** (interpretation of the labels, kept close to the wording):
- **Relevant** = the source relates to *your* research question.
- **Zitierfähig** (citable) = the source *can be reliably found/retrieved* by the reader (formal criterion).
- **Zitierwürdig** (worth citing) = the source is *scientifically credible* (quality criterion), judged by five indicators: trustworthy author; methodical, structured approach; a scientific question; quality assurance by the publication medium; precise scientific language and references to other sources.

A source should pass **all three** questions to be used as scientific support (this "all three" reading is the knowledge-base author's interpretation of the figure's title "Wissenschaftlichkeit … bewerten"; the figure itself does not state an aggregation rule).

## Part B – What the booklet says (ZBW/EconBiz, 2025)

### B1. What is a good scientific source? (p. 17)
> Stütze Dich bei einer wirtschaftswissenschaftlichen Arbeit überwiegend auf Artikel aus Fachzeitschriften (S. 19) und aktuelle Working Paper (S. 18) aus Forschungseinrichtungen. Diese findest Du in Fachdatenbanken (S. 15).

**CRAAP test** (inspired by Sarah Blakeslee) — *„Ist es Mist oder eine gute Quelle? Mache den CRAAP-Test – vor allem in Zweifelsfällen. Bei Publikationen, die Du in Fachdatenbanken findest, erübrigt sich das."* (p. 17, verbatim criteria):

| Criterion | Question (verbatim) |
|---|---|
| **Currency** | Sind die Informationen aktuell? Erfordert das Thema aktuelle Informationen oder sind evtl. auch ältere Quellen geeignet? |
| **Relevance** | Bezieht sich die Information auf Dein Thema? Richtet sich das Paper an die wissenschaftliche Fachcommunity? |
| **Authority** | Wer hat das Paper verfasst? Sind die Autor:innen Expert:innen auf dem Gebiet? An welcher wissenschaftlichen Einrichtung sind sie tätig? |
| **Accuracy** | Sind die Informationen durch Referenzen belegt? Wurden die Informationen geprüft oder begutachtet (Peer Review)? |
| **Purpose** | Ist der Text an wissenschaftlicher Erkenntnis orientiert und mit neutraler Haltung geschrieben? Oder werden andere Ziele verfolgt (politische, finanzielle, Lobby-Arbeit, Propaganda, …)? |

### B2. Peer review (p. 16)
Quality assurance procedure common for journals: peers from the same community review an article before publication. **„Wenn mehrere Fassungen einer Publikation vorliegen, zitiere die Fassung, die ein Peer Review durchlaufen hat."**

### B3. Working papers (p. 18)
Published by research institutes, usually freely online; **no peer review**, but valued for being current and *„in den Wirtschaftswissenschaften wichtige Quellen"*. Questions: Is it issued by a reputable research institution? Has it since been published as a journal article? → **then cite the reviewed article instead.** Identifying features (annotated example, Kiel Working Paper): *Forschungsinstitut, Serienbezeichnung, Zählung innerhalb der Serie*.

### B4. Journals and rankings (p. 19)
Journals publish articles on specific topics several times a year. Journal rankings show which are most read/cited:
- Web of Science – Journal Citation Reports (library licence)
- **VHB-Rating 2024** (German business-administration ranking)
- Scimago Journal Rank
- IDEAS/RePEc Simple Impact Factors for Journals
- Journal Weights List (Handelsblatt-Ranking)

Bibliographic anatomy shown: *Zeitschriftentitel, Band/Volume, Heft/No./Issue*, DOI (example: Rossini et al., *Agricultural and Food Economics* (2017) 5:1, DOI 10.1186/s40100-016-0070-9).

### B5. Textbooks (p. 16)
> Lehrbücher sollten nicht die Basis Deiner Hausarbeit sein. Sie geben einen Überblick über Forschungsstand und Themen, präsentieren aber keine originalen Forschungsergebnisse. … Aber: Lehrbücher können einen guten Einstieg bieten für eine Übersicht zu einem Thema oder zu Definitionen von zentralen Begriffen. Auch im Falle von Methoden oder bestimmten Formeln (z. B. aus Statistik und Ökonometrie) dürfen durchaus Lehrbücher zitiert werden.

### B6. Review articles (p. 12)
*Übersichtsarbeiten* (systematic reviews, literature reviews/surveys) present the current state of research — good entry points.

### B7. Search tools and what they return (p. 14)
Google = websites, not for scholarly search (only to get into the topic); Google Scholar = scholarly, but ranking favours highly cited (often older) work; library catalogue = vetted sources; subject databases = first choice.

### B8. Wikipedia & lexica (p. 13)
Wikipedia and subject lexica (e.g. Gabler Wirtschaftslexikon) are named **as sources for search terms** — the booklet does not endorse them as citable evidence.

### B9. AI tools (p. 8, 16)
ChatGPT-type tools sometimes invent sources → not suitable for reliable search; verify every AI-supplied source in databases. AI tools are not authors and are not listed in the bibliography (p. 8).

### B10. Critical reading (p. 20)
*„Übernimm Literatur nicht unkritisch in Deine Arbeit, sondern bewerte sie. Beachte auch den Hintergrund/die mögliche Absicht, die hinter einem Artikel steht."*

### B11. Volatile sources (p. 28)
Websites and other changeable sources: *„sichere diese vollständig ab."*

## Part C – Additions (knowledge-base author; general knowledge, not from source unless page given)

### C1. Practical "Is this source citable?" checklist

Run in this order; stop at the first hard failure.

1. **Relevant?** (Prexl) — Does it support a specific claim tied to the TF's research question? If it only provides background colour → drop or shorten.
2. **Zitierfähig?** (Prexl) — Can a reader reliably find exactly this version?
   - Has complete bibliographic data (author/organisation, year, title, publisher/journal, pages) and ideally a DOI/ISBN/stable URL?
   - Web source: URL + access date + archived copy (booklet p. 28).
   - Company-internal document: NAK has a specific procedure for internal sources → see [Zitieren interner Quellen.pdf](<../originals/Zitieren interner Quellen.pdf>). Unpublished internal sources are generally not publicly retrievable and need special handling (e.g. appendix, documented provenance).
3. **Zitierwürdig?** (Prexl's five indicators, made operational):
   - *Vertrauenswürdiger Autor:* identifiable author with expertise / institutional affiliation (= CRAAP *Authority*, p. 17).
   - *Methodisches, strukturgeleitetes Vorgehen:* method and data described; results traceable.
   - *Wissenschaftliche Fragestellung:* aims at knowledge, not at selling/lobbying (= CRAAP *Purpose*).
   - *Qualitätssicherung durch Publikationsmedium:* peer review, editorial review, reputable publisher/institute (= CRAAP *Accuracy*, p. 16).
   - *Präzise Wissenschaftssprache & Verweise:* uses defined terminology and cites its own sources (= CRAAP *Accuracy*).
4. **Current enough?** (CRAAP *Currency*, p. 17) — Fast-moving topics (IT, regulation, AI, markets) need recent sources; classic theory/definitions may be old.
5. **Best available version?** — Peer-reviewed version instead of preprint/working paper (p. 16, 18); primary source instead of secondary quotation where obtainable.
6. **Role in the argument appropriate?** — A weak source may still be cited *as an object* (e.g. "Hersteller X behauptet…", a company's self-description, a market figure) but not as proof of a scientific claim.

### C2. Source-type tier list (for Wirtschaftsingenieurwesen TFs)

Tiers express default trust for **supporting scientific claims**; the checklist above can move a single source up or down. Booklet references show where a tier is backed by the source.

| Tier | Source type | Use in a TF |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Peer-reviewed journal articles (ideally ranked, e.g. VHB/SJR) (p. 16–17, 19); systematic reviews/meta-analyses (p. 12) | Core evidence and state of research |
| **2** | Scholarly monographs and edited volumes from academic publishers; conference papers with peer review; norms/standards (DIN, ISO, VDI) | Core evidence; standards are authoritative for definitions/requirements |
| **3** | Working papers of reputable research institutes (p. 18); official statistics and reports of public bodies (Destatis, Eurostat, OECD; see [research_data.md](research_data.md)); laws/regulations | Current data and findings; check for later journal version (p. 18) |
| **4** | Textbooks / Lehrbücher (p. 16) | Definitions, overviews, methods/formulas only — not the basis of the argument |
| **5** | Reputable practitioner literature: industry association studies, consultancy studies (McKinsey, Fraunhofer-practice reports, etc.), trade journals (Fachpresse), quality newspapers | Context, practice relevance, recent figures; flag possible interests (CRAAP *Purpose*) |
| **6** | Company-internal documents, interviews, own data | Legitimate as **empirical material/object of study**, not as scientific theory; cite per NAK internal-source rules |
| **7** | Company websites, white papers, vendor/marketing material, press releases | Only as statements of that company ("laut Hersteller…"); never as neutral proof |
| **8** | Wikipedia, general lexica, blogs, forums, social media, YouTube, AI chatbot output | Not citable as evidence. Wikipedia/lexica only for finding terms (p. 13); AI output never a source/author (p. 8) and AI-suggested references must be verified (p. 16) |

### C3. Red flags an AI reviewer should raise
- Reference has no author **and** no institution, or no year ("o. J.") without justification.
- Majority of references are websites/tier 7–8; few or no journal articles (contradicts p. 17).
- A textbook carries the main argument (p. 16).
- A working paper is cited although a published journal version exists (p. 18).
- Reference cannot be found in Google Scholar / a database / DOI resolver → possible hallucinated or mis-cited reference (p. 16).
- Single-source claims for central statements; sources listed side by side without being related or weighed (booklet p. 26).
- Statistics without original publisher (e.g. Statista cited instead of the underlying survey).

## Part D – NAK's own source rules (binding; take precedence over Parts B/C)

**Sources:** [converted_faq-2305.md](<../originals/converted_faq-2305.md>) ("Literatur/ interne Daten") · [Beispielhafte Komentare.pdf](<../originals/Beispielhafte Komentare.pdf>) §2, §10 · [Transferleistung_Tipps zur Anfertigung.pdf](<../originals/Transferleistung_Tipps zur Anfertigung.pdf>) p. 2 · [Bewertungsschema](<../originals/Bewertungsschema Transferleistungen.pdf>) criterion II.8 (K.O.)

- **Databases (FAQ):** NAK library databases via CIS. **EBSCO** is the general recommendation (filter "scholarly peer-reviewed journals" + "full text"). For CS: **IEEE, ACM, SpringerOpen** (all peer-reviewed). Examiners also point to Google Scholar, beluga (Hamburg libraries) and ACL Anthology.
- **Journal quality (FAQ):** check the ranking. **VHB-JOURQUAL** (A+ to D; "k.w.Z." = keine wissenschaftliche Zeitschrift) is the reference list: <https://vhbonline.org/vhb4you/vhb-jourqual/vhb-jourqual-3/gesamtliste>. Look for the impact factor and peer-review statement on the journal homepage.
- **Internet sources (FAQ):** allowed, but *"Es dürfen nur nicht ausschließlich Internetquellen genutzt werden."* NAK's quality ladder, in descending order (verbatim):
  - a) Umfassend, weitgehend vollständig, Literatur relevant für Thema der Arbeit, fast ausschließlich aktuelle Primärliteratur.
  - b) Umfangreich, wenig „Füllliteratur", hauptsächlich Primärliteratur.
  - c) Ausreichende Anzahl von Quellen, viele allgemeine Quellen (Füllliteratur), hauptsächlich Sekundärliteratur (Bücher, Internetquellen)
  - d) Wenige Quellen, Literatur wenig relevant für Thema der Arbeit, überwiegend Sekundärliteratur (Bücher, Internetquellen)
- **Not acceptable / not scientific (examiners):** commercial and advertising websites, press releases, blogs, business lexica (*Wirtschaftslexika*), company articles, news sites (*"selbst manchmal weniger renommierte Zeitungen"*), Medium.com, Wikipedia (Tipps p. 2: *"Achtung vor Internetblogs, Lehrbüchern, wikipedia"*). If such a source is unavoidable, **justify its use in the text**.
- **Recency (one examiner):** *"Als ‚veraltet' gilt generell alles, das älter als drei Jahre ist."* Even for fundamentals, prefer recent high-impact peer-reviewed studies over textbooks. Classic foundational works (e.g. Porter) remain citable as *Grundlagenwerke*; the rubric's 1,7 band explicitly allows *"angemessene Grundlagenwerke"*.
- **Mix:** secondary literature (papers rather than books) dominates at Bachelor level. Add primary sources (own interviews, surveys, data) for impact. *"Eine gute Arbeit sollte beides enthalten."*
- **Lecture scripts:** grey literature, not citable (see [internal_data_and_interviews.md](internal_data_and_interviews.md)).
- **Don't miss the obvious:** an examiner criticised ignoring a meta-study that was *"einer der ersten Hits auf Google Scholar"*. Always run the plain Google Scholar search for the topic's key terms.
- **Rubric thresholds (II.8, K.O.):** textbooks and internet sources only, without the current state of research → 3,7–4,0. Peer-reviewed journals plus the current state of research, with all claims backed → 1,7–2,3. See [grading_rubric.md](grading_rubric.md).

## Reviewer checklist (apply to a TF draft)

For each reference in the bibliography and each central claim:
- [ ] **Relevant:** clearly tied to the research question (Prexl; CRAAP Relevance p. 17).
- [ ] **Zitierfähig:** complete bibliographic data; DOI/ISBN/stable URL; web sources with access date and archived; internal sources handled per NAK rules (Prexl; p. 28).
- [ ] **Zitierwürdig:** identifiable expert author/institution, transparent method, scientific purpose, quality assurance (peer review/editorial), precise language with references (Prexl; CRAAP p. 17).
- [ ] **Current** enough for the topic (CRAAP Currency p. 17).
- [ ] Peer-reviewed version preferred over working paper/preprint (p. 16, 18).
- [ ] Source mix: mostly journal articles/working papers from databases; textbooks only for definitions/methods (p. 16–17).
- [ ] No Wikipedia, blogs, AI chat output as evidence; AI-suggested references verified (p. 8, 13, 16).
- [ ] Interest-driven sources (vendors, lobby groups, consultancies) are labelled as such and not used as neutral proof (CRAAP Purpose p. 17).
- [ ] Sources are evaluated critically and related to each other, not just strung together (p. 20, 26).
