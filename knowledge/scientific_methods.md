## 1. What a Transferleistung must achieve methodologically

Core statements from NAK (Methoden INF):

- "Der Fokus einer wissenschaftlichen Arbeit liegt auf der Anwendung von Forschungsmethoden." (Methoden INF, S. 3)
- "Sie sollten also weniger versuchen, die Welt durch neue Ergebnisse zu revolutionieren, sondern gesichertes Wissen durch die korrekte Anwendung von Forschungsmethoden in einer angemessenen Zeit produzieren." (S. 3)
- "Jede wissenschaftliche Arbeit ist die Antwort auf eine offene Frage" – "Fragen Sie sich, welches Problem Sie unter Anwendung welcher wissenschaftlichen Methodik lösen wollen?" (S. 3)
- Diagram on S. 3: **Fragestellung aus Unternehmen** + **Anwendung Forschungsmethoden** + **Literatursichtung / Bewertung / Auslegung** → **Transferleistung**.
- Forschungsmethoden "beschreiben, wie die Untersuchung eines Objektes durchgeführt wird" – "Wie" as a process; the research object is analysed for characteristics; goal: investigate "wissenschaftlich, strukturiert und nachvollziehbar" to obtain "gesicherte Erkenntnisse". Data are collected via interviews, surveys or other methods "und es muss nachvollziehbar dokumentiert werden, wie diese erhoben worden sind." (S. 4)
- "Sehen Sie sich eher als Wissenschaftlicher als ein Mitarbeiter im Betrieb." (S. 22) / "Vermeiden Sie Berichtcharakter" (S. 27)
- "Achtung: Nicht eigene Erfahrungen in den Mittelpunkt stellen – immer subjektiv – zentraler Teil ist die objektive Analyse einer Problemstellung mithilfe wissenschaftlicher Methoden." (S. 26)
- "Nicht das Rad neu erfinden – es geht darum, dass Sie auf vorhandenem Wissen aufbauen und dieses in einen Kontext setzen und damit erweitern" (S. 27)

## 2. Science and research – basic concepts

### 2.1 What science is (Schneider)
- **[ppt image, Folie 4]** "Science is… Creation and Discovery of Knowledge · Refinement of Knowledge · An open-ended, indeterminate process"; Natural Sciences = "Explaining natural laws"; Social Sciences = "Explaining and understanding social lawlike phenomena and phenotypes"; Humanities = "Understanding ideas".
- **Science Checklist** (Folie 5): focusses on natural/social world or ideas; uses testable/replicable theories; relies on evidence; involves the scientific community; open-ended process; "No moral judgement, no aesthetic judgement: science does not tell people how to use scientific knowledge"; specifics of economics/business: **descriptive, normative, prescriptive, prognostic**.
- **[ppt image, Folie 6] Research vs. Methodology vs. Methods:**
  - *Research*: "investigation or experimentation aimed at the discovery and interpretation of facts, revision of accepted theories or laws in the light of new facts, or practical application"
  - *Methodology*: "deals with general approaches or guidelines to conducting research; provides the principles for organizing, planning, designing, and conducting research"
  - *Methods*: "provide the specific details of how one accomplishes a research task (procedures and methods); provides specific and detailed procedures of how to initiate, carry out, and complete a research task by mainly focusing on how to do it"
- Learning goals of the lecture (Folie 2): differentiate deductive, inductive, quantitative, qualitative inquiry; formulate statements, hypotheses, research questions; **differentiate between the factual and the normative**; reference following a standard (MLA, Chicago, APA); know research-ethics/compliance standards, which vary by context.

### 2.2 Good vs. bad research (Schneider, Folie 8–9)
| Bad research | Good research |
|---|---|
| not focussed, bad data, unclear goals, isolated, bad language, "opinio-nated", means vs. ends (confused), no structure, no theory, no real-world link | objective, original, truthful, replicable, good data, reliable, well written, relevant/useful, valid, clear |

### 2.3 Gütekriterien (quality criteria)
Methoden INF S. 4: "Wie gesichert der Erkenntnisgewinn ist, hängt von Gütekriterien ab":
- **Objektivität** – "kommen andere zum gleichen Ergebnis?"
- **Reliabilität** – "bleiben Ergebnisse stabil und bei wiederholter Messung gleich?"
- **Validität** – "Gültigkeit und Genauigkeit, die gewählte Methode"
- "Ihre Datenerhebung sollte immer objektiv sein"

The library deck points to handbooks for "Objektivität, Reliabilität und Validität" (Bibliothek WM, S. 3) and to Mayring for "Gütekriterien" of qualitative analysis (S. 5).

### 2.4 Paradigms and epistemology (Schneider, Folie 40–43, all [ppt image])
- **Alternative terms:** Positivistic paradigm = quantitative / objectivist / scientific / experimentalist / traditionalist; Phenomenological paradigm = qualitative / subjectivist / humanistic / interpretivist. Continuum "Approach to social sciences" from Positivist to Phenomenologist: reality as concrete structure → concrete process → contextual field of information → realm of symbolic discourse → social construction → projection of human imagination.
- **Positivistic vs. phenomenological** (Folie 41):

| Positivistic | Phenomenological |
|---|---|
| quantitative data | qualitative data |
| large samples | small samples |
| hypothesis testing | generating theories |
| data highly specific and precise | data rich and subjective |
| location artificial | location natural |
| reliability high | reliability low |
| validity low | validity high |
| generalises from sample to population | generalises from one setting to another |

- **Induktion** (Tatsache → Theorie, Rudolf Carnap) · **Falsifikation** (Theorie → Tatsachen, Karl Popper) · **Paradigma** (Theorie A → Theorie B, Thomas S. Kuhn) (Folie 43).
- Hypothesis testing logic (Folie 21): negative test → hypothesis rejected → theory rejected; positive test → hypothesis cannot be rejected → theory cannot be rejected. "«cannot be rejected» does not prove «truth»" … "means: the theory works as a working horse". Data can also be used for parameter estimation and prognosis (Folie 22).

### 2.5 Argument quality (Schneider [ppt image], Folie 31 "Analysis / Writing")
- Occam's razor: "Entities should not be multiplied unnecessarily" – "When you have two competing theories that make exactly the same predictions, the simpler one is the better".
- **Argument:** "Conclusions follow naturally from assumptions and reasoning".
- **Cohesion:** "The way in which writing 'sticks together'" – "Are the parts of the written argument clearly linked to one another?" – "Always link data / examples to model and theory".
- **Consistence:** "Definitions clear", "Uniform usage of terms", "Uniform style".

## 3. The research process

### 3.1 The cycle (both decks show the same idea)
- Methoden INF S. 6 "Vorgehen einer wissenschaftlichen Arbeit" (circular diagram): **Forschungsfrage → Sichtung Literatur → Methode – wie will ich Frage lösen? → Daten sammeln → Auswertung / Analyse → Ergebnisse →** (back to Forschungsfrage).
- Schneider [ppt image, Folie 7]: **Research question → Survey of Literature → Design → Collection of Data → Analysis → Findings →** (back). This is also the lecture's chapter structure (Folie 3).

### 3.2 The TF-specific flow
Methoden INF S. 8 "Ablauf": *Fragestellung im Unternehmen* → "Worin könnten Lösungen bestehen?" → "Daten sammeln, Hypothesen aufstellen" → "Methode (Experiment, Interview, etc.) zur Validierung: Wie beantworte ich nachvollziehbar und begründet die Frage?" → **"Handlungsempfehlung"**.

Methoden INF S. 23 "Wie schreibe ich eine Transferleistung? – Transfer Wissenschaft und Praxis" (four boxes):
1. "Thema im Betrieb absprechen und Lösungsstrategien entwickeln" – "Eignet sich das Thema? Finde ich ausreichend **Quellen**? Wie ist der aktuelle **Forschungsstand**?" *(phase: Auftragsklärung)*
2. "Wie, mit welcher **Methode**, kann ich meine Forschungsfrage lösen?" – "Sehen Sie sich als Wissenschaftler, der objektiv ein Ergebnis erzielt, und **begründen** Sie Ihr Vorgehen" – "→ **Herleitung** zu den im Studium erlernten Inhalten" *(Auftragsklärung)*
3. "Daten sammeln und **Auswertung** der Quellen mithilfe von wiss. Methoden (Literatur, interne Daten, Interviews, Progammcodes, Nutzwertanalyse, Statistiken, etc.)" *(Arbeit verfassen, parallel Selbstreflexion)*
4. "Ergebnisse der Analyse zusammenfassen → Handlungsempfehlung für Praxis ableiten → Welche **Rückschlüsse** sind daraus zu ziehen?"

### 3.3 "Erste Überlegungen" before starting (Methoden INF S. 7)
I. Define scope – "welche Art von Ergebnis, denn danach richtet sich die Findung der geeigneten Methode / Framework / Referenzmodell"
II. "Was ist mein Ziel? Daraus Forschungsfrage ableiten"
III. Requirements for the experiment / framework?
IV. Get familiar with fundamentals ("bildet Theorieteil")
V. "Daten sammeln, Hypothesen aufstellen (Fehler einplanen)"
VI. Perspective: "Entwickler, Kunde, User, Projektmanager, Wissenschaftler, …?"
VII. "Literaturbasis prüfen"
VIII. "Hypothesen / Handlungsempfehlungen (Aussagen, die Sie beweisen möchten)"
IX. **Order:** "Beschäftigen Sie sich zuerst mit den wissenschafts-theoretischen Erkenntnissen. Danach anhand Ihres neuen Wissensstandes mit den Gegebenheiten Ihres Unternehmens (interne Unterlagen, Interviews, Auswertungen Programmcodes, …)." (repeated on S. 25) – and be clear "warum wird das Experiment / Studie durchgeführt? Wie wird es durchgeführt?"

### 3.4 Research design – choosing what kind of text you write (Schneider)
**[ppt image, Folie 19]** three design questions: "What kind of research / text will it be?" → "Which (managerial / economic) models, theories apply?" → "What kind of data do you need? How do you collect data?"

Schneider distinguishes four text/design types (Folie 20–26). How each is built is the backbone of the method catalogue below:

| Type | The RQ is answered with… | Build (Folie 26) |
|---|---|---|
| **Empirical** (Folie 20) | a **hypothesis** tested with data ("If…, then…", "The more…, the more (less)…"; dependent = what is explained, independent = what explains) | Formulate hypothesis based on theory → collect data → analyze data |
| **Analytical** (Folie 23) | a **thesis** ("a proposition … to be discussed and proved or to be maintained against objections") | Thesis based on theory → sort pros and cons incl. supporting facts → craft a string of arguments → conclusion |
| **Comparison** (Folie 24) | a **comparison** of at least two objects using criteria ("rules or principles for evaluating something") | Define objects → define criteria (explain why chosen) → (weight criteria) → apply criteria to each object using data/examples → summarize |
| **Essay** (Folie 25) | development of a (new) theory or model; uses examples/case studies to make the case | "Make the point – use examples and data to persuade". Warning: "Often, essays are not scientific texts, since they have persuasive objectives". |

"Must: at least one dedicated chapter / section" on design, including the "Decision about what (managerial/economic) model, or theory to use" (Folie 26).

## 4. Topic and research question

### 4.1 Finding a topic (Methoden INF S. 5)
- Brainstorming: "Womit beschäftigen Sie sich gerade im Unternehmen? / Was interessiert Sie?"; links to the curriculum ("Blick ins Modulhandbuch"); read trade journals, collect information, make lists.
- "Von einem generellem Thema zu einer spezifischen Fragestellung"
- Parameters: "Zeitvorgabe; Länge, Relevanz, Was benötige ich?"

### 4.2 Sharpening the question (Methoden INF S. 12 "Fragestellung schärfen")
Types of research questions, with NAK's own examples – useful to classify a RQ and pick a method:

| Question type (verbatim) | Example from slide |
|---|---|
| "Beschreibung eines Zustands oder der Realität" | Digitalisierung im Unternehmen |
| "Begründung von Zusammenhängen und Ursachen" | Effizienz von Mitarbeitern in Zusammenhang mit Software |
| "Gestaltung von Lösung" | Referenzmodell zur Einführung von Software |
| "Prognose und Hypothesen" | Projektmanagement 2030 |
| "Evaluation einer Tatsache" | Evaluation der Einführung von Software |
| "Vergleiche von Datenbanken, Software, etc." | – |

- **Title rule:** "aus zwei Teilen zusammengesetzt: Was wird getan und wie wird es getan?" – example: **„Agilität in KMU – Befragung der Mitarbeiter im Unternehmen"** (S. 12).
- "Sie verändern Forschungsmethoden leicht, indem Sie Ihr eigenes Interview, eigene Kriterien aufstellen"
- Ask questions of the topic ("Was fasziniert/irritiert mich?"), change perspective ("Cluster-Technik").
- "Thema in Frageform fassen → präzisiert Frage → macht sichtbar, unter welcher Perspektive: (Welche Chancen/Risiken?)"

### 4.3 The research-question funnel (Schneider [ppt image], Folie 10)
Inverted triangle, three levels:
1. "General idea: what interests you? What are the components of your broad field of interest? Can you identify issues, topic and themes?"
2. "What are the gaps in literature? What deserves being critically called into question? Which real-world problems are there that can be adressed with scientific methods?"
3. **"Research question – in one sentence"**

### 4.4 Where the RQ goes – the introduction (Schneider, Folie 11)
"Ideally: in an introduction":
1. Define general topic
2. What research has already been conducted
3. What are gaps in literature (the main finding of the next section)
4. **Define your specific research question – in one sentence**
5. How will you answer your question (the main structure of the section on design)
6. Order of remaining paper

### 4.5 Bad research questions (Schneider, Folie 12 exercise)
The deck asks "What is wrong with these research questions?":
- "How to make managers better?"
- "Is sales-growth the right measurement for the value-creation of a firm?"
- "What is the difference between the Chinese and the German exports?"

The slide gives no answer. *(General knowledge, not from source:)* typical diagnoses are – too vague/normative and not operationalisable ("better" undefined); yes/no question without criteria or theory; too broad, no criteria or scope for a "difference". Use this list to test student RQs: one sentence, specific object, specific criterion/perspective, answerable with a named method in ~10 pages.

### 4.6 Auftragsklärung questions (Methoden INF S. 13)
To clarify "vorher im Betrieb" ("inhaltlich ist Ihr Betreuer für das Thema verantwortlich"):
- Bietet das Thema interessante oder neue Aspekte?
- Ist die Quellenlage ausreichend?
- Welches Hintergrundwissen ist zusätzlich erforderlich?
- Ist eine Bearbeitung in der vorgesehenen Zeit und in der vorgegebenen Länge möglich?
- Ist der Praxisbezug sichergestellt?
- "Wie möchte ich vorgehen, um die Fragestellung zu lösen? Welche Methode wende ich an? Verknüpfen mit Inhalten aus dem Studium"
- "Mögliche erste Ergebnisse / Arbeitshypothesen festhalten"

The Auftragsklärung is the "Bauplan" to orient on (S. 27).

## 5. Working with literature

### 5.1 Purpose
- Methoden INF S. 9: build on other researchers' findings; summarise them in a Literaturanalyse ("Related works"); then contribute through your own data collection and analysis; "Führen Sie auf der Basis der Erkenntnisse aus der Literatur die Diskussion fort; selten untersuchen Sie eine Lücke".
- Schneider Folie 14: learn about research and answers to related questions (read broadly); learn how a body of research evolved; display your understanding; identify important works, points of agreement/consensus, points of disagreement/controversy, areas for further research; "Providing readers with the intellectual context and some motivation for your original research (narrow focus)".

### 5.2 Search technique
- Methoden INF S. 9: databases (ACM, Google Scholar, Open Access, IEEE, etc.); sensible keyword combinations, "Schneeballsystem nutzen", Boolean AND/OR/NOT; targeted Google Scholar search with a renowned journal name (e.g. Digitale Transformation "MIS-Quarterly"); phrase search with quotes ("Process Mining"); review literature from good articles.
- Schneider Folie 16 databases: EBSCO, ScienceDirect, JSTOR, RFE, Google Scholar, NORDAKADEMIE library.
- **[ppt image] Literature survey process (Schneider, Folie 15):** Browse your research topic and question → Identify similar texts → Prioritize recent texts → Find out the «Classics» → Summarize.
- Library (Bibliothek WM, S. 6): "Book a Librarian" – individual Zoom appointment on digital resources, citable sources, Citavi, **methods**, "Thema schärfen", plagiarism/citation rules (bibliothek@nordakademie.de).

### 5.3 Source quality
- Methoden INF S. 10: scientific articles – quality markers **peer review / impact factor** (e.g. VHB JOURQUAL list for Wirtschaftsinformatik); conference proceedings and working papers also suitable; books – only some peer-reviewed, often outdated research except standard works; caution with internet blogs; practice-oriented trade journals can help to find current discussions; "renommiert und praxisnah ist die HMD – Praxis der Wirtschaftsinformatik (verfügbar über WISO)".
- Name the source quality in the text: „Ein konkretes Beispiel ist DHL: Das Unternehmen bestätigt laut einem Artikel in einer Fachzeitschrift …" (S. 10)
- Always ask: "Was war das Ziel/der Kontext des Autors? Wie hat der Autor geforscht? Wie lauten die wichtigsten Ergebnisse in Bezug auf meine Forschungsfrage?" (S. 10)
- **[ppt image] Schneider source tiers (Folie 13):** *Scientific Articles* – published after peer review, in journals with impact factor; also acceptable: working papers, conference proceedings. *Books* – usually not scientific literature; some undergo peer review; edited volumes often better than monographs; often outdated. *Other Articles* – not scientific literature; usable for orientation; might help in identifying research questions.
- Schneider Folie 17 "Caution!": "Primary literature trumps secondary literature · Published trumps unpublished · Scientific journals trumps any other".

### 5.4 "Ich finde keine Literatur" (Methoden INF S. 11)
Possible reasons: topic too new; too specific (broaden); not scientifically relevant, purely a practice topic; not studied in this context. Remedies: "um die Ecke denken" – change context (e.g. agility not in finance but in production); transfer from other fields or general findings and adapt; **Grounded Theory** (work without literature, generate own qualitative data, build theory of an unknown phenomenon); discuss the difficulties in the Selbstreflexion and show critical handling of sources.

## 6. Data (Schneider, Folie 28–29)

**[ppt image, Folie 28]** classify every data set by **Source** (primary / secondary) · **Type** (quantitative / qualitative) · **Test / Usage** (causation / correlation / description / exemplification).

"How to incorporate data in your work?" (Folie 29) – ideally a dedicated chapter/section:
- "In the early stages of the Bachelor curriculum: stick to secondary sources"
- "… stick to exemplification and descriptive statistics"
- "… try to combine qualitative and quantitative research"
- Always understand what the data is about, where it comes from, how it was collected – "**State this awareness in your paper**"
- "Data alone does not tell anything. It must be interpreted – with the help of a theory."

Methoden INF S. 15: qualitative research = explorative, often small data sets (e.g. expert interviews), for individual or unknown questions, open but still clearly structured and justified procedure; disadvantage: subjective, interpretive, "angreifbar". Quantitative = standardised, structured data capture; many data sets needed; advantage: generalisability, comparability; e.g. online surveys. "Nach der Sammlung von Daten gilt es, diese so auszuwerten, dass gesichertes Wissen in Form von Hypothesen vorliegt."

## 7. Method catalogue

### 7.0 Method map

### 7.1 Literaturanalyse / Literature review
- **What:** "In einer Literaturanalyse wird vorhandenes Wissen sinnvoll zusammengefasst. Dadurch wird zwar primär kein neues Wissen generiert. Das bereits vorhandene Wissen lässt sich auf diese Weise strukturieren, sodass darauf aufgebaut werden kann." (Methoden INF S. 17)
- **When:** "Literaturstudie steht i.d.R. am Beginn jeder Forschung" – i.e. part of *every* TF; as the sole method: "Reine Literaturanalyse hat Ziel, Bedarf an Forschung in Themenfeld aufzuzeigen" (S. 17).
- **Goal:** capture state of research and/or technology to gain "Überblick über das Themengebiet und Auffinden von einer oder mehreren ‚Lücken' im Themengebiet" (S. 22).
- **How / document:**
  - Search systematically (§5.2) and document the procedure: "Die Vorgehensweise muss nachvollziehbar sein! Das bedeutet neben dem Nennen der zugrunde liegenden Quellen, auch die Vorgehensweise genauestens zu beschreiben." Test: "kann eine Person, die noch nie etwas mit der Arbeit zu tun hatte, mit der Beschreibung die Literaturrecherche durchführen und kommt zu den gleichen Ergebnissen?" (S. 22) → state databases, search terms/combinations, period, selection criteria. *(The concrete list of items is general knowledge, the requirement itself is from source.)*
  - Schneider Folie 17: dedicated chapter; organise **either** one paragraph per study **or** one paragraph per theme; evaluate each source; identify conflicts in the literature and gaps between real-world application and literature; **summarize gaps**; "Create a bridge between the gaps identified here, your question and the design of your research."
  - Writing (Folie 32): paraphrase, use direct quotes sparingly; give more important studies more detail; signal importance by noting influence on subsequent studies.
- **Pitfalls:** listing sources without evaluating them; no bridge to own method; reliance on blogs/Wikipedia/textbooks (S. 24); not stating search procedure; "Rad neu erfinden" instead of building on existing knowledge (S. 27).

### 7.2 Experteninterview / qualitative interview
- **What:** "Ausgewählte Experten zu Hypothesen und Problemstellungen zu befragen" (Methoden INF S. 16). Qualitative, behavioural (S. 14).
- **When:** individual or unknown questions where little data exist, explorative (S. 15); to link theory with company practice.
- **How:**
  - "Offene Fragen, aus Erkenntnisse der Literatur Fragebogen konzipieren" – the guide is derived *from the literature*.
  - "Rolle des Experten genau definieren"
  - "Umso mehr, desto repräsentativer die Ergebnisse"; **Sättigungskriterium**: reached "wenn nach einer gewissen Menge an Wiederholungen keine signifikant neuen Ergebnisse gewonnen werden"
  - "Transkription oder Gesprächsprotokoll (unterschreiben lassen)"
  - "Auswertung: definierte Aspekte" – Excel or software like MAXQDA ("lohnt sich erst ab zehn oder mehr")
  - "Erläutern Sie Ihr Vorgehen und stellen das Interview als die geeignete Methode heraus"
  - Literature: Baur/Blasius, *Handbuch Methoden der Empirischen Sozialforschung* (Springer 2019) (S. 16); Gläser/Laudel *Experteninterviews und qualitative Inhaltsanalyse* and Helfferich *Die Qualität qualitativer Daten* for "Planung Experteninterview; Leitfaden; Grundlagen empirische Sozialforschung"; Mayring *Qualitative Inhaltsanalyse* for "Auswertung/Analyse exploratives, sprachliches Datenmaterial; Gütekriterien"; Misoch *Qualitative Interviews* (Bibliothek WM, S. 4–5).
- **Document:** interview guide, expert selection and role, date/setting, transcript or signed protocol (in appendix), evaluation categories. *(Putting transcripts in the appendix: consistent with the current formal requirements doc, see cross-references.)*
- **Pitfalls:** subjectivity and "angreifbar" (S. 15) – needs discussion of limits; too few experts with no saturation argument; questions not grounded in literature; not justifying why an interview and not another method.

### 7.3 Befragung / Umfrage / Fragebogen (survey)
- **Source coverage:** named as quantitative behavioural method (Methoden INF S. 14), "Onlinebefragungen" as quantitative example (S. 15), "Umfrage" in method list (S. 25), title example „Agilität in KMU – Befragung der Mitarbeiter im Unternehmen" (S. 12). Schneider: sampling, structured interviewing, self-completion questionnaires (Folie 42). Literature: Porst *Fragebogen*, Kallus *Erstellung von Fragebogen* ("Praxisbuch → Entwicklung Fragebögen mit Beispielen"), Hollenberg *Fragebögen*, Jacob *Umfrage*, Klandt *Empirische Forschungsmethoden in der BWL*, ZIS Open-Access-Repository for measurement instruments (Bibliothek WM, S. 4–5).
- **When:** standardised data, many data sets; advantage generalisability/comparability (S. 15).
- **How / pitfalls** *(general knowledge, not from source)*: derive items from theory/hypotheses; pre-test; state population, sample, response rate; report descriptive statistics (consistent with Schneider's advice to stick to descriptive statistics early on, Folie 29); in-company samples are small – avoid overgeneralising; leading or double-barrelled questions; put the questionnaire in the appendix.

### 7.4 Experiment (incl. Performanceanalyse / benchmark)
- **What / why (Methoden INF S. 19):** "Experimente machen einen wichtigen Teil des Erkenntnis- und Theoriebildungsprozesses aus". An experiment means: obtain or build an implementation; connect to an optimisation problem, set parameters; run (several times), record and evaluate results. Purpose: define/determine different conditions; show that it works; check e.g. whether an algorithm meets requirements; for complex/randomised/heuristic procedures predictions are hard.
- **Measures:** comparisons of performance, resource use, RAM, disk space, etc.; for algorithms false positive / false negative; define metrics ("wie hoch ist Erkennungsrate?"); Messdaten (S. 19).
- **Execution/report structure (S. 20 "Durchführung Experiment"):**
  1. Main question to clarify
  2. Pre-experimental planning: first tests leading to the objective
  3. Objective: precise (scientific) thesis and derived (statistical) hypotheses
  4. Setup: problem design, algorithm design, quality measure (Gütemaß), start and end condition etc.
  5. Result/visualisation: the most important data and first graphs
  6. Observations: deviations from expected result or striking patterns, **"aber keine Bewertung"**
  7. Discussion: results of statistical tests and the (subjective) classification/evaluation of results and observations
  - **"Konzentrieren Sie sich auf ein Experiment."**
- **NAK worked example (S. 21)** – „Untersuchung von Schema Evolution verschiedener Datenbanktypen für Business Process Management Systeme am Beispiel vom Unternehmen X", method: Experiment. Structure: 1. Grundlagen (Schema Evolution, Workflow Engines, relationale DB, Graph-DB) · 2. Abgleich mit Literatur · 3. choose two DBs, representative examples (SQLite, Neo4j), **justify choice** · 4. run experiment, measure under defined aspects · 5. define and justify measurements (runtime, RAM, disk) · 6. record and assess anomalies · 7. Handlungsempfehlung. → This is exactly the TF analysed in [example_good_tf_informatik.md](<example_good_tf_informatik.md>).
- **Pitfalls:** no justification for the chosen objects/metrics; single run (no repetition); mixing observation and evaluation; unfair comparison set-up (the example TF itself discusses this); too many experiments for 10 pages.

### 7.5 Comparison / Kriterienkatalog / Nutzwertanalyse
- **Source coverage:** Schneider's "Comparison" design (Folie 24, 26) is the methodological core; "Kriterienkatalog" and "Nutzwertanalyse" are named as NAK example methods (Methoden INF S. 23, 25); "Vergleiche von Datenbanken, Software, etc." as a question type (S. 12); "eigene Kriterien aufstellen" (S. 12).
- **How (Schneider Folie 24/26):** define the objects of comparison (≥ 2) → choose criteria **and explain why they were chosen** → optionally weight criteria → examine each object in light of each criterion using data or examples → summarize findings.
- **Nutzwertanalyse** *(general knowledge, not from source)*: multi-criteria scoring – define alternatives and (ideally literature- and stakeholder-derived) criteria, weight criteria (sum 100 %), score each alternative per criterion on a defined scale, compute weighted sums, rank, run a sensitivity analysis on weights.
- **Document:** source of criteria (literature, requirements, interviews), weighting rationale, scoring scale definitions, the full scoring matrix (appendix if large), who scored.
- **Pitfalls:** criteria/weights pulled from thin air; no argument why the criteria are relevant; presenting the result as objective although weights and scores are subjective. *(Cross-source note: a NAK examiner comment in `Beispielhafte Komentare.pdf` explicitly criticises not discussing the drawbacks of chosen methods, "bspw. eine nur scheinbare Objektivität einer Nutzwertanalyse" – see that file's knowledge entry.)*

### 7.6 Analytical thesis / argumentation (Schneider)
- **What:** RQ answered with a thesis, defended against objections (Folie 23).
- **How:** Thesis → argument in favor → supporting fact → argument against → supporting fact → counter-balancing argument → fact … → conclusion; thesis based on theory; "Sort 'pros' and 'cons' including supporting facts → Craft a string of arguments" (Folie 26). Schneider's practice task (Folie 33): RQ, thesis, review of 3 publications, one managerial/economic model, **two arguments and two counter-arguments**, secure findings.
- **When:** questions of "Begründung von Zusammenhängen" or evaluation where data collection is limited; literature-heavy TFs.
- **Pitfalls:** "opinio-nated" research (Folie 8); no counter-arguments; arguments without facts; confusing factual and normative statements (Folie 2).

### 7.7 Fallstudie / case study
- **Source coverage:** qualitative behavioural (Methoden INF S. 14); "Case studies" (Schneider Folie 42); essays "use examples, case-studies" (Folie 25).
- *(General knowledge, not from source:)* in-depth study of one (or few) real cases in context using multiple data sources (documents, interviews, observation); in a TF typically "am Beispiel des Unternehmens X". Pitfalls: becomes a descriptive report ("Berichtcharakter", S. 27) without analytic frame; overgeneralisation from one case.

### 7.8 Gruppendiskussion / Focus groups
- **Source coverage:** named only (Methoden INF S. 14; Schneider Folie 42 "Focus Groups").
- *(General knowledge, not from source:)* moderated discussion of several participants on a guide; captures shared views/disagreements; document participants, guide, protocol; pitfalls: dominant participants, hierarchy effects in company settings.

### 7.9 Beobachtung / Observation
- **Source coverage:** named (Methoden INF S. 25; Schneider Folie 42: "Structured Observations" (quant.) and "Observations" (qual.)).
- *(General knowledge, not from source:)* systematic recording of behaviour/processes using a predefined observation scheme; document scheme, period, observer role; pitfall: observer bias, own role in the company.

### 7.10 Prototypen / Design Science
- **Source coverage:** qualitative, design-oriented: "Prototypen", "Design Science (Softwareprototypen entwickeln)" (Methoden INF S. 14); "Gestaltung von Lösung" as RQ type (S. 12).
- *(General knowledge, not from source:)* build an artefact (software prototype, model, concept) to solve a defined problem, derive requirements from literature and stakeholders, then **evaluate** the artefact against those requirements (test, expert feedback, experiment). Pitfall: a TF that only describes "what I built" without requirements derivation and evaluation is a project report, not research.

### 7.11 Aktionsforschung / action research
- Named only (Methoden INF S. 14). *(General knowledge, not from source:)* researcher intervenes in the practice setting in iterative cycles (plan–act–observe–reflect) and studies the effects; fits dual students who change processes in their own team; must make own role and bias explicit.

### 7.12 Referenzmodellierung / reference modelling
- Named as quantitative design-oriented method (Methoden INF S. 14); "Referenzmodell zur Einführung von Software" as example RQ (S. 12); "Methode / Framework / Referenzmodell" as possible result types (S. 7).
- *(General knowledge, not from source:)* develop or adapt a generic model (process, data, architecture) from literature and cases, then apply/validate it in the company.

### 7.13 Simulation
- Named (Methoden INF S. 14; library handbooks cover simulation, Bibliothek WM S. 4). *(General knowledge, not from source:)* model a system and run scenarios; document model assumptions, parameters, validation against real data.

### 7.14 Grounded Theory
- Methoden INF S. 11: option when no literature exists – "Bearbeitung des Themas ohne Literatur, Sie erzeugen eigene (qualitative) Daten → Theoriebildung eines unbekannten Sachverhalts". Discuss the difficulty in the Selbstreflexion.

### 7.15 Content analysis / document & secondary-data analysis
- Schneider Folie 42: content analysis, secondary analysis and official statistics (quant.); collection and qualitative analysis of texts and documents; discourse/conversation analysis (qual.). Methoden INF S. 23/25: "interne Unterlagen", "interne Daten", "Auswertungen Programmcodes", "Statistiken". Qualitative Inhaltsanalyse after Mayring (Bibliothek WM S. 5).
- Schneider Folie 29: for early bachelor work, secondary sources and descriptive statistics are recommended; always state data origin and collection.
- *(General knowledge, not from source:)* define category system before coding (deductive) or derive it from the material (inductive); for internal documents use the NAK rules for citing internal sources and Sperrvermerk.

### 7.16 Strategic/analysis tools: SWOT, TRIZ, Umweltanalyse, FMEA
- **Source coverage:** only as library pointers – "Analysemethoden Überblick z.B. SWOT, TRIZ Umweltanalyse, FMEA" with handbooks: Nagel *Methodenhandbuch der Betriebswirtschaft*, Kerth *Die besten Strategietools in der Praxis*, Kreutzer *Toolbox für Marketing und Management*, Paul *Instrumente des strategischen Managements*; plus the "Methodenportal" (Bibliothek WM, S. 3).
- *(General knowledge, not from source:)* these are analysis/structuring tools, not data-collection methods. A SWOT needs evidence for every entry (from interviews, data, literature) – otherwise it is opinion. FMEA rates failure modes by severity/occurrence/detection (RPN). TRIZ = systematic inventive problem solving. Umweltanalyse (e.g. PESTEL) structures external factors. In a TF, state where the inputs come from (the actual *method*) and use the tool to structure/evaluate them; cite a methodological source for the tool.

### 7.17 Anforderungsanalyse (requirements analysis)
- **Not covered** by these three sources. *(General knowledge, not from source:)* elicit requirements (interviews, document analysis, workshops), structure them (functional / non-functional, prioritisation e.g. MoSCoW), validate with stakeholders; often the first step of a Design-Science or Nutzwertanalyse TF (requirements become evaluation criteria).

## 8. Justifying the method (Methodenbegründung)

This is an explicit grading criterion (Methoden INF S. 25) and an examiner tip (S. 27). The slide "Methode begründen" (S. 18):
1. "Begründung Ihres Vorgehens ein wichtiges Kriterium: Warum haben Sie diese Methode gewählt und wodurch grenzt sich diese von anderen Methoden ab bzw. wie hätte eventuell ein anderes Vorgehen die Ergebnisse verändert?"
2. "Vor- und Nachteile von Forschungsmethoden erläutern und diskutieren"
3. "Zeigen Sie, dass die gewählte Methode die beste für Ihr Vorgehen ist"
4. "Nutzen Sie die Selbstreflexion und schreiben auf, was Sie beim nächsten Mal anders gemacht hätten → Kommunikation Prüfer"

Also: cite a methodological source for the method itself ("definierten Forschungsmethoden, die nachvollziehbar sein sollten (Quellen belegen)", S. 14); Schneider requires at least one dedicated design chapter (Folie 26).

**Template for a method section** (synthesised from S. 18, 20, 22, 25 and Schneider Folie 26; wording is a suggestion):
1. Which RQ type (§4.2) and which design (empirical / analytical / comparison) → therefore which method.
2. Why this method fits, with methodological source; which alternatives were considered and why rejected; what another method would have changed.
3. Concrete procedure: objects/sample/experts, criteria/metrics/guide, data collection, evaluation procedure – reproducible by a stranger.
4. Known limitations of the method (subjectivity, small n, weighting arbitrariness, set-up fairness) and how they are mitigated.

## 9. Writing, structure and findings

### 9.1 Elements and structure of a paper (Schneider)
- Elements (Folie 35): Title, Author, Abstract, Keywords, (if requested) TOC / table of figures / abbreviations, **Introduction (special status)**, Literature Review, Theoretical Framework, Main section, Findings, References, Appendix.
- Structure (Folie 36): decimal numbering (1, 1.1, 1.1.1); "Don´t go «too deep»; usually three layers more than enough; try to stick to two"; "Strive for symmetry, ie. equally long subsections"; **every table and every graph needs a reference**; TOC not necessary in short texts *(NAK TF formal docs may require it – check)*; **"Introduction and conclusion are never more than 20% of the paper"**.
- References (Folie 37): usually in-text, less usually footnotes; "APA and Harvard are well regarded in business and economics"; every publisher has its own style.

### 9.2 Mechanics of writing (Schneider, Folie 32)
Direct quotations sparingly, paraphrase; prioritize important studies; short paragraphs – "One paragraph, one idea"; paragraph = topic sentence + elaboration + supporting evidence; subheadings clarify structure; "Imagine that you are telling a story".

### 9.3 Findings / Fazit (Schneider, Folie 34)
Restate the research question; restate hypothesis / thesis / criteria and objects / main point; summarize results; "Did you confirm your hypothesis? Explain how so, or why not."; weaknesses of your study; areas for further study; **"No new citations, tables, or footnotes"**.
NAK adds: Handlungsempfehlung in the Fazit, evaluate it, show transferability to other companies (Methoden INF S. 26).

### 9.4 Weasel phrases to avoid (Schneider, Folie 44 "Caution")
Satirical translations – useful for review comments (typos of the slide silently corrected):
"It is believed → i believe" · "It is generally believed → some buddies and I believe" · "It has long been known → I have no idea where this quote comes from" · "In my experience → once" · "In case after case → twice" · "In a series of cases → thrice" · "Preliminary experiments showed that → i hope, that" · "Several lines of evidence demonstrate that → it would fit our purpose" · "A definite trend is evident → the data is useless" · "While it has not been possible to provide definite answers … → our experiment does not work but we hope the article to be published anyway" · "This is correct within an order of magnitude → it´s wrong" · "Additional work is required → I have nothing / I don´t understand it" · "This study confirms and expands previous conclusions → we have nothing new".

### 9.5 NAK examiner tips (Methoden INF S. 27 "Stolperfallen / Prüfertipps")
- Narrow the topic sensibly – keep the question with practice relevance in view → **roter Faden**
- Follow your "Bauplan" → Auftragsklärung
- Use the Selbstreflexion to name weaknesses/problems → communication with examiner
- Question source quality, check scientific nature
- Don't reinvent the wheel – build on existing knowledge
- Avoid "Berichtcharakter" – act as a scientist; objectivity through data analysis
- Justify: "Warum ist genau diese Methode zielführend und nicht eine andere?"
- Care with sources, texts, data – cite correctly and **uniformly**; don't pass off others' ideas as your own
- Spelling/grammar; avoid colloquial language ("man"); get proofreading
- Correct directories, formatting – plan enough time

### 9.6 Research ethics (Schneider, Folie 38)
**Honesty** (no fabrication/falsification/misrepresentation), **Objectivity** (avoid bias in design, analysis, interpretation; disclose personal/financial interests), **Carefulness** (avoid careless errors; keep good records of data collection, design, correspondence), **Respect for Intellectual Property** (no unpublished data/methods without permission; credit all contributions; never plagiarize). *(For a TF: disclose your own role in the company as a potential bias; general knowledge, consistent with "Objectivity".)*

## 10. Method selection decision aid

Built from the RQ types (Methoden INF S. 12), design types (Schneider Folie 20–26) and method map (Methoden INF S. 14). Method suggestions per row beyond what the slides pair explicitly are *(general knowledge, not from source)*.

| If the research question is about… (S. 12 type) | Design (Schneider) | Candidate methods | Typical TF result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Describing a state ("Beschreibung eines Zustands") | descriptive / empirical | Literaturanalyse + Experteninterview, Befragung, document/data analysis, Beobachtung, Fallstudie | Ist-Analyse, gap vs. literature → Handlungsempfehlung |
| Explaining causes/relations ("Begründung von Zusammenhängen") | empirical (hypothesis) or analytical (thesis) | Befragung (quant.), Experteninterviews (qual.), secondary data; or thesis with pro/contra | Hypothesis confirmed/rejected, explained |
| Designing a solution ("Gestaltung von Lösung") | design-oriented (Konstruktivwissenschaft) | Prototyp / Design Science, Referenzmodellierung, (Anforderungsanalyse) + evaluation | Artefact/concept + evaluation |
| Forecast ("Prognose und Hypothesen") | empirical / prognostic | Experteninterviews (e.g. scenario-like), trend/secondary data, Simulation | Prognosis with stated assumptions |
| Evaluating something ("Evaluation einer Tatsache") | comparison against criteria, or empirical | Kriterienkatalog, Nutzwertanalyse, Befragung of users, KPI/data analysis | Evaluation result + recommendation |
| Comparing tools/DBs/software ("Vergleiche") | comparison | Kriterienkatalog, Nutzwertanalyse, Experiment / Performanceanalyse | Ranked/justified choice |
| Algorithm/system behaviour, performance | empirical (experiment) | Experiment with metrics, Simulation | Measured results + recommendation |
| Topic without literature | qualitative, theory-building | Grounded Theory, Experteninterviews | Initial theory/model; flag in Selbstreflexion |
| Strategic position of a product/unit | analytical / comparison | SWOT, Umweltanalyse **fed by** interviews/data/literature | Strategy recommendation |

Quick filter questions:
1. **What kind of result do I need?** ("welche Art von Ergebnis, denn danach richtet sich die Findung der geeigneten Methode", S. 7)
2. **Is there literature?** No → broaden/change context or Grounded Theory (S. 11).
3. **Is quantitative data obtainable in 4 weeks in sufficient amount?** No → qualitative (interview) or experiment with own data (S. 15; *time limit S. 8*).
4. **From which perspective?** (developer, customer, user, PM, scientist – S. 7)
5. **Can I justify it against an alternative?** (S. 18)
6. **Can a stranger repeat it and reach roughly the same result?** (S. 22, 25)
7. **Does it fit 10 pages?** "Konzentrieren Sie sich auf ein Experiment" (S. 20); Schneider recommends secondary sources, descriptive statistics and combining qual./quant. early in the bachelor (Folie 29).

## 11. Method review checklist (for reviewing a TF draft)

**Research question & intro**
- [ ] A research question is stated explicitly, ideally in one sentence, in the introduction (Schneider Folie 10–11).
- [ ] RQ is specific, answerable, has a perspective; not a vague normative or yes/no question (Methoden INF S. 12; Schneider Folie 12).
- [ ] Title = "Was wird getan" + "wie wird es getan" (S. 12).
- [ ] Intro covers topic → existing research → gap → RQ → how it will be answered → order of paper (Folie 11).
- [ ] Company problem is the starting point ("Fragestellung aus Unternehmen", S. 3, 8).

**Literature (K.O.)**
- [ ] Current, scientific sources (peer-reviewed journals, proceedings), not mainly blogs/Wikipedia/textbooks (S. 10, 24; Schneider Folie 13, 17).
- [ ] Literature is evaluated and synthesised (consensus, controversy, gaps), not just listed (Folie 14, 17).
- [ ] Explicit bridge: gap → RQ → method (Folie 17).
- [ ] Search procedure reproducible if a literature analysis is the method (S. 22).
- [ ] Theory first, then company situation (S. 7, 25).

**Method**
- [ ] Method named and explained with a methodological source (S. 14).
- [ ] Choice justified; alternatives considered; pros/cons discussed (S. 18, 25).
- [ ] Dedicated method/design section exists (Schneider Folie 26).
- [ ] Procedure is reproducible (Objektivität) (S. 4, 25).
- [ ] Design type is clear: hypothesis (empirical), thesis (analytical), criteria (comparison) (Folie 20–26).
- [ ] Interview: guide derived from literature, expert role defined, number/saturation argued, transcript/protocol, defined evaluation aspects (S. 16).
- [ ] Experiment: question, hypothesis, setup, metrics justified, repeated runs, observations separated from evaluation, focus on one experiment (S. 19–20).
- [ ] Comparison/NWA: objects, criteria with justification, weights justified, applied consistently; subjectivity acknowledged (Folie 24).
- [ ] Data origin, type and collection stated (Schneider Folie 28–29); internal documents/interviews/evaluations verifiable (appendix).

**Results, discussion, Fazit**
- [ ] Results presented with tables/figures that are referenced in the text (Folie 36).
- [ ] Gütekriterien / limitations discussed (S. 4, Folie 34).
- [ ] Fazit restates RQ, answers it, states weaknesses and further research, **no new sources/tables** (Folie 34).
- [ ] Handlungsempfehlung for the company, evaluated, transferability shown (S. 26 – K.O. "Theorie auf Praxis angewendet und reflektiert").
- [ ] Not centred on personal experience; no "Berichtcharakter" (S. 26, 27).

**Form**
- [ ] ~10 pages ±10 % (S. 8; check current rules); intro + conclusion ≤ ~20 % (Folie 36).
- [ ] Max. 2–3 heading levels, balanced subsections (Folie 36).
- [ ] Uniform citation style (S. 27); no "man"/colloquial language; no weasel phrases (Folie 44).
- [ ] Sperrvermerk if internal data (S. 8).
- [ ] Selbstreflexion names the method's weaknesses and what would be done differently (S. 8, 18, 27).
