# Citation styles for NAK Transferleistungen — overview, style choice, cross-source conflicts, review checklist

This folder holds AI-usable references for the citation guides collected for NORDAKADEMIE Transferleistungen (TF/TL).

**Conventions used in all files**
- Rules are taken from the source documents.
- Anything added from outside is marked *(general knowledge, not from source)* or *(suggestion / derived)*.
- Examples are copied verbatim, in German.
- Page refs point to the original PDF; each file states its page-numbering convention.

**Conclusion:**
- NAK accepts any style that is customary in the subject („übliche Zitierweise aus dem jeweiligen Fachgebiet“), as long as it is **applied uniformly**.
- No accepted-styles list exists in any document in this repo.
- NAK-wide requirements on top of any style: an alphabetical Quellenverzeichnis sorted by author, not split into categories; only cited sources; access dates for web sources; no laws in the list; internal sources and interviews listed and attached in the appendix.

### Recommendation logic (Wirtschaftsingenieurwesen)
1. **Pick ONE style and ONE edition** for the whole TF and apply it everywhere, including internal sources and interviews.
   - The most frequent reviewer complaint is inconsistency or an unrecognisable style (*Beispielhafte Komentare*, p. 7).
2. **MLA** is defensible but sits outside the business/engineering mainstream.
   - Its own guide places it in „Sprach-, Sozial- und Kulturwissenschaften“ (mla.md); Harvard calls itself the style of „Wirtschafts- und Sozialwissenschaften“ (harvard.md); Schneider rates APA/Harvard as well regarded in business.
   - If keeping MLA, decide whether it is **standard MLA 9** `(Scholz 45)` or the **IMF variant** `(Scholz 2014: 45)`. The IMF guide's bibliography format (with „Print.“) is MLA-7-era and won't match Citavi/Zotero "MLA" output.
3. **Author-date styles (APA, Harvard, IMF-MLA)** fit NAK's „alphabetisches Quellenverzeichnis … nach Autoren sortieren“ directly.
4. **IEEE** conflicts with the literal NAK checklist (alphabetical list), even though NAK's own good-TF example in computer science uses it. Prefer it only for clearly IT/engineering topics.
5. **Footnote styles (DIN/THI, Träger)** match NAK's *Tipps* wording about footnotes (p. 4) and the FAQ's internal-document template (identical to DIN no. 28).
6. Whatever the choice, cite company sources following the **content** rules of [internal_sources.md](internal_sources.md), rendered in the chosen style's **format**.

## Discrepancies between the sources (know these before reviewing)

1. **Interviews and e-mails in the reference list:** NAK FAQ, Träger and DIN say **yes**; the Harvard guide (p. 34) says **no**. For NAK: **list them**.
2. **Can internal or grey sources be cited?** The NAK FAQ quotes Sandberg („nicht zitierfähig … graue Literatur darf … nicht verwertet werden“, with an exception for explorative/qualitative studies) and then explains how to cite them via the appendix. Träger: citable **only as data sources**, never for the scientific part.
3. **Sort order for the same author:** DIN = newest first; APA, Harvard and MLA = oldest first.
4. **Order inside multiple citations:** APA = alphabetical; Harvard = by importance, then chronological.
5. **et al. thresholds** differ (APA 3/6 rule, Harvard 3+, MLA >3, DIN/IEEE list all).
6. **Block-quote threshold:** APA 40 words; Harvard 3 lines; MLA „40 Zeilen“ (likely a typo).
7. **E-book marking:** DIN marks `[online] … PDF e-Book`; the NAK FAQ says marking it as an e-book is not needed.
8. **Edition text:** DIN copies the full edition statement („nach Vorlage“: „6., neubearbeitete und erweiterte Auflage“); Harvard says leave out such add-ons or abbreviate; APA wants changes shown („4., neu bearb. Aufl.“).
9. **„ff.“:** Harvard says avoid it; DIN and MLA allow it.
10. **IEEE vs NAK:** IEEE uses citation order; NAK asks for an alphabetical list. NAK's own good-TF example uses IEEE anyway, and puts `[n]` after the period, contrary to the IEEE guide.
11. **Footnotes:** NAK *Tipps* p. 4 talks about footnotes, but NAK-cited material also endorses in-text styles (Schneider: „Usually, in-text reference“). Either is fine if consistent.
12. **Outdated versions:** APA guide = 6th ed. (current: 7th); MLA guide = MLA-7-era bibliography (current: 9th); IEEE electronic formats from 2005; Träger cites § 17 UWG (replaced by GeschGehG 2019). *(The current-version facts are general knowledge.)*
13. **Each guide has internal inconsistencies** (wrong years between text and list, etc.). They are listed at the end of each file. Don't copy a guide's example blindly; follow its stated rule.

## AI tools (not covered by any of the six guides)
- NAK requires an **Eigenständigkeitserklärung** and a **Prozessdokumentation** (prompt, date, purpose, quality assessment, tool/version) whenever AI was used.
  - [2024_10Eigenständigkeitserklärung KI.pdf](<../../originals/2024_10Eigenständigkeitserklärung KI.pdf>): AI-generated parts must be marked and documented.
  - [NAK_Prozessdokumentation_KI_final_20250219.pdf](<../../originals/NAK_Prozessdokumentation_KI_final_20250219.pdf>): the template, with entries like „ChatGPT 4.0“ and „19.02.2025; vgl. S. 5“.
  - FAQ: if no AI was used, the FAQ recommends a note in the first footnote saying so. Undeclared use = Täuschung.
- The ZBW booklet (p. 8): „KI-Tools sind keine Personen im Sinne des Urheberrechts. Sie stehen nicht als Autor:innen im Literaturverzeichnis …“. So document AI use in the Prozessdokumentation, not as a bibliography source. Facts taken from AI output must be verified and cited to real literature (NAK example: „Quellen nicht zitierwürdig, u.a. wikipedia“).

## Citation review checklist (for an AI reviewer applying it to a TF draft)

**A. Style identification and consistency**
1. Can the style be identified (APA / Harvard / MLA-IMF / MLA 9 / DIN-footnote / IEEE / Träger)? Name it. If not: flag „Kein klar definierbarer Zitationsstil“.
2. Is it used uniformly in every in-text citation **and** every list entry? Look for mixed separators (`:` vs `,`; `S.` vs none), mixed `und`/`&`, mixed German/English labels (S./p., Hrsg./Ed., Aufl./ed., Verfügbar unter/Available), mixed date formats, and mixed final periods.
3. Is there only one citation mechanism (in-text **or** footnotes), not both? (In Harvard, footnotes are for remarks only.)

**B. Coverage: what must be cited**
4. Every statement that is not common knowledge, not your own derivation or result, and not logically entailed needs a citation. That includes company facts.
5. Every paraphrased paragraph carries its own citation, or a scope note such as „Vgl. (auch im Folgenden)“ (NAK Tipps p. 4) and the citation ends with the paragraph.
6. Every figure or table taken or adapted from elsewhere has a source line. Own figures need none (NAK Tipps p. 4). The list of figures contains no sources (Kommentare p. 6).
7. Interviews mentioned anywhere (including the Selbstreflexion) are cited in the text and listed.

**C. In-text form**
8. Direct quotes have a page (or Absatz/Min./Folie for internal sources) and use correct „…“ quotation marks. Changes are marked with `[...]`, `[sic]`, `[Hervorhebung …]`, `[Anm. d. Verf.]`.
9. Paraphrases follow the chosen style's convention (vgl./Vgl. in Harvard/DIN/Träger; none in APA/MLA/IEEE), with a page wherever specific content is paraphrased.
10. Author counts and et al. follow the chosen style's thresholds. Same-year works have a/b in both the text and the list.
11. Second-hand quotes use „zitiert nach“, and only the secondary source is listed.
12. Verbatim quotes are sparse („wörtliche Zitate sehr sparsam einsetzen“, NAK Tipps p. 4). Block-quote formatting matches the style's threshold. English quotes are not translated; other languages get a translation in [ ] marked as one's own, without naming oneself (FAQ, double-blind).

**D. Reference list (Quellenverzeichnis)**
13. 1:1 match: every in-text source is in the list and every list entry is cited. No "further reading".
14. One list, alphabetical by author (NAK), **not split** into categories (books/online/internal). IEEE is the exception: it uses citation order, so flag the conflict with NAK p. 5.
15. Every entry is complete for its type:
    - books: author, year, title, edition (2nd+), place, publisher;
    - chapters: chapter author + title, editors (Hrsg.), volume title, pages;
    - articles: journal, volume, issue, pages, DOI if the style uses DOIs;
    - web: URL + **access date**.
    - The reader must be able to tell the source type (Kommentare p. 7).
16. Author names in full and correctly ordered per style; all authors listed where the style requires it.
17. No laws in the list (NAK Tipps p. 3). Cite §§ in the text or footnote.
18. Source quality: no or very few blogs, encyclopedias (Lexika), Wikipedia, news or company PR in place of scientific literature (Kommentare p. 6). Not exclusively internet sources (FAQ). Lecture scripts are not citable.

**E. Internal sources (see [internal_sources.md](internal_sources.md))**
19. Internal sources are used **only as data sources**, never for theory or definitions.
20. Each internal source is identifiable: company/author, title, ID/version, date, author function, approval/container, storage location/URL + access date, and **an appendix reference**.
21. Every internal document, database extract, e-mail and interview transcript cited is **in the appendix**, or a footnote explains why it can't be, as agreed with the company (FAQ). Sperrvermerk set if needed.
22. Interview transcripts or minutes: date, interviewee, function/position, topic, **signature** of the interviewee; **the student's name does not appear** (double-blind).
23. No uncitable items: oral instructions, personal notes/memos, presentations with unclear author or date.

**F. AI and integrity**
24. If AI was used: a Prozessdokumentation is present and the referenced pages match; no AI tool listed as an author. If no AI was used: an optional note in the first footnote (FAQ).
25. No passages reused verbatim from one's own earlier TFs. Earlier TFs are cited via the appendix („Anhang X, Transferleistung 6, S. xx“).
